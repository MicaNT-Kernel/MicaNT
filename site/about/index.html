The Manifesto: Reclaiming Digital Autonomy

Somewhere over the past twenty years, the personal computer stopped belonging to the person who bought it.

Modern mainstream operating systems have devolved into bloated surveillance delivery vehicles. They ship with background telemetries that cannot be disabled, forced updates that overwrite user configurations, ad networks baked directly into taskbars, and memory footprints exceeding 8 gigabytes of RAM just to idle at a desktop. A computer running on modern consumer hardware spends more computational power analyzing the user than executing the user’s code.

MicaNT was born as an absolute rejection of this paradigm. It is built on a single, unbending covenant: your hardware belongs to you. No telemetry. No phone-home diagnostics. No forced reboots. An operating system executive that idles in less than 32 megabytes of RAM, boots in milliseconds, and executes every cycle in service of the human sitting in front of the screen.

The Dave Cutler DEC MICA & PRISM Heritage

MicaNT is not a Linux distribution with a theme, nor is it a repackaged hypervisor. It is an independent, clean-room resurrection of Dave Cutler’s original 1988 DEC MICA & PRISM architecture—the architectural predecessor to the original Windows NT executive.

Cutler’s original systems design was arguably the most elegant computing architecture of the late 20th century:

The Object Manager (ob/)
A single, handle-based, atomic-counted namespace uniting every file, driver, thread, and physical device under uniform security descriptors.
Asynchronous Completion (IOCP)
True non-blocking I/O queues allowing a single thread to drive millions of packet transfers without thread starvation or priority inversion.
Structured Exceptions (SEH)
Two-pass exception dispatching with zero-cost table-based unwinding, preserving complete CPU state during unexpected faults.

MicaNT takes these pure principles and reimagines them in modern ISO C++23—leveraging compile-time type safety, concepts, strict RAII resource lifetime management, and zero legacy 16-bit/32-bit compatibility cruft.
